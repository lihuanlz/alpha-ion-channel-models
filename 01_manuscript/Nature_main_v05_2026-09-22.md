## Abstract

Cardiac ion-channel models underpin in silico drug-safety assessment, yet the standard route, global fitting of Markov schemes to voltage-clamp data, yields non-identifiable parameters. Here we invert the workflow: for four cardiac channels (hERG/$I_{\mathrm{Kr}}$, $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$/$I_{\mathrm{Na}}$, $Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$/$I_{\mathrm{CaL}}$, KCNQ1+KCNE1/$I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$) we extract gating time constants, steady-state availability and delays from public voltage-clamp data as voltage-dependent tables, test each for constancy, and assemble models with one free per-cell conductance and, where data demand it, one midpoint shift. Time-constant tables prove portable across cells (CV 0.13--0.19, hERG deactivation; 0.029, $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ inactivation at threshold); voltage midpoints do not. The pattern repeats in all four channels and in an independent five-temperature programme (670 wells). Without global fitting, the models forward-predict held-out protocols, including action-potential clamp and 65 of 68 CiPA drug-screening traces. A Fisher-information audit of a 13-parameter Markov model shows the data identify only modal coordinates (six null directions; condition number $7.4 \times 10^{12}$). In all four channels drugs reject pure pore block and modulate steady-state occupancy and inactivation tables, not kinetics. A fifth channel ($K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$) reproduces all published anchors; a candidate condition-dependent inactivation-timescale shift is registered. Every governing quantity is either measured directly or an explicitly registered assumption; cell-to-cell variability is localised, not averaged away.

## Main

Cardiac ion-channel models sit at the centre of the Comprehensive In Vitro Proarrhythmia Assay (CiPA) paradigm, in which simulated action potentials under multi-channel drug block inform regulatory decisions on torsadogenic risk^1--3^. The dominant modelling practice fits Hodgkin--Huxley-type or Markov-state schemes to voltage-clamp recordings by global optimisation^4,5^. Two difficulties with this practice have become increasingly clear. First, the fitted parameters are typically not identifiable: independent fits to the same data return different rate constants, and systematic identifiability analyses of ion-channel models repeatedly find large flat directions in parameter space^6--8^. Second, averaging procedures across cells, laboratories and protocols conceal real biological variability and, for fast currents such as $I_{\mathrm{Na}}$, can introduce biases comparable to reported disease-mutation effect sizes^9^. Model parameters that cannot be tied to any single measured quantity also cannot be audited: when a simulation disagrees with experiment, there is no localisable locus of failure.

An alternative is possible because modern voltage-clamp datasets are protocol-rich. The same cells are now routinely subjected to activation, inactivation, deactivation, sinusoidal, staircase and action-potential-clamp protocols within a single recording session^10--13^. Under such coverage, the quantities that actually govern channel behaviour (how fast activation and inactivation proceed at each voltage, where steady-state availability sits, how long opening is delayed) can be read out of the data directly, one voltage at a time, without any commitment to a state diagram. We call this construction the $\alpha$-model: every dynamic element is a measured table, and the test of the model is not the quality of a fit but the forward prediction of protocols never used in its construction, on cells never fitted.

Here we apply this pipeline to the four channels that dominate cardiac repolarisation and excitability and the CiPA channel panel: hERG ($I_{\mathrm{Kr}}$), $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ ($I_{\mathrm{Na}}$), $Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$ ($I_{\mathrm{CaL}}$) and the KCNQ1+KCNE1 complex ($I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$). For each channel we ask three questions: which kinetic quantities are constant enough to be carried across cells; which must remain per-cell; and how do drugs move the measured tables. The answers turn out to be structured. Time constants are portable across cells; steady-state midpoints are not, and must be treated as per-cell biological coordinates. Drugs act on steady-state and inactivation tables, not on kinetics, in all four channels, but the number of separable action components is channel-specific. One channel ($Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$) refuses the treatment in a precise and informative way, and we report that failure as a quantitative result: its inactivation time constant at $-40$ mV is not a number but a broad distribution across constructs and laboratories. Finally, we show that the extracted hERG and $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ components can be dropped into the O'Hara--Rudy human ventricular model^14^ in place of their Markov counterparts without degrading action-potential morphology or drug-response scoring, while making every governing quantity individually auditable. As a portability test beyond cardiac channels, we further apply the pipeline to a fifth channel, $K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$ ($I_{\mathrm{to}}$-family A-type current), in the only public protocol-rich dataset available^22^.

### Results

**The extract--judge--assemble pipeline**

The pipeline has three stages (Fig. 1a). In the *extract* stage, each protocol family is reduced to voltage-dependent tables by local, protocol-specific measurement: mono- or bi-exponential time constants of activation, inactivation and deactivation; steady-state activation and availability curves with midpoints and slopes; activation delays; reversal potentials. No global fit is performed at any point. In the *judge* stage, each table is subjected to a pre-registered constancy test: a table is accepted as a channel-level constant only if its cell-to-cell dispersion is statistically indistinguishable from measurement noise, across cells, batches and, where available, laboratories and temperatures (with temperature handled as an explicit covariate). Tables that fail are not discarded; they are re-registered either as per-cell quantities or as distributions with a median and a spread, and the verdict (constant, per-cell, distributed, or unusable) is recorded in a judgement card before any prediction is attempted. In the *assemble* stage, accepted tables are inserted into a Hodgkin--Huxley-form current equation, the per-cell free parameters are enumerated explicitly, and the model is required to predict, without retuning, protocols and cells held out from construction (Fig. 1b).

Two features of this design are essential. First, because every table is a direct measurement, the provenance of every number in the model is traceable to named cells and named sweeps; disagreement with experiment can be localised to a single table at a single voltage. Second, the judgement stage converts what is usually implicit averaging into explicit verdicts: where the field's practice is to average away cell-to-cell differences, we measure them, report their magnitudes, and carry only what survives.

**hERG: zero-fit construction predicts sinusoid, action-potential and drug-screening protocols**

For hERG we used the published sine-wave dataset of Beattie et al.^10^ (nine cells, each recorded under activation, inactivation, deactivation, sinusoidal, staircase and action-potential-clamp protocols) and, independently, three CiPA automated-patch-clamp laboratory datasets (68 drug-screening traces). The assembled current has the form

$$I = G\, m\, h\, (V - E_{\mathrm{rev}}), \tag{1}$$

with a single reversal potential $E_{\mathrm{rev}} = -88.33$ mV shared by all protocols and cells (Fig. 2a).

Three deactivation time constants (fast, middle and late components) are constant across the nine cells with coefficients of variation 0.13--0.19 (Fig. 2b), indistinguishable from measurement noise, and are therefore carried as channel-level tables. A frequently assumed simplification, that the inactivation gate is fully open at negative voltages (h = 1 below $\approx$ $-70$ mV), is directly refuted by the data: measured steady-state availability at $-70$ mV is 0.42, and we therefore carry the full twelve-point measured availability table rather than any analytic approximation. Activation exhibits a voltage-dependent delay of 50--150 ms that must be represented explicitly (Methods; SI S1).

The model was assembled using only the traditional square-step protocols of each cell, with per-cell conductance G as the sole free parameter, and was then required to forward-predict the held-out protocols. It reproduces the sinusoidal-protocol current in 7 of 9 cells (78\%; 95\% Wilson CI 45--94\%) and the action-potential-clamp current in 8 of 9 cells (89\%; 95\% CI 57--98\%) at the pre-registered accuracy threshold (Fig. 2c--d; Extended Data Fig. 1). It reproduces the dovetailed CiPA drug-screening traces in 65 of 68 cases (96\%; 95\% CI 88--99\%) across three laboratories (lab 3: 20/20; lab 4: 16/16; lab 5: 29/32), and the residuals of its deactivation prediction pass a whiteness test in 28 of 33 tail segments (SI S2). We note that these predictions are made with a single free per-cell number, against datasets and protocol families entirely disjoint from construction. The nine-cell base is the only manual-patch dataset carrying all six protocols in the same cells; we therefore report exact binomial intervals rather than bare fractions, and we corroborate across the three independent CiPA laboratories rather than within the construction set. As an external population-level check we then extracted the same modal quantities from an independent automated-patch dataset of 211 QC-passed CHO cells at 25 $^{\circ}$C, each recorded under nine protocols^18^ (with the authors' manually curated 124-cell subset scored in parallel). The population-level signatures reproduce: per-cell reversal potentials are constant across cells ($-83.9$ mV median, CV 0.024, n = 209; curated subset CV 0.017, n = 124), the negative limb of steady-state inactivation is cell-invariant ($h_{\mathrm{ss}}$ = 1.000 at $-140$ mV and 0.955 at $-120$ mV, CV $\le$ 0.065), and the deactivation ladder ordering is preserved (0.74 s at $-60$ mV versus 3.03 s at $-40$ mV). Forward prediction of the held-out action-potential-clamp recordings at 0.5/1/2 Hz, with only per-cell G and $E_{\mathrm{rev}}$ carried over, reproduces current magnitude in 91--99\% of cells (frequency-dependent; 100\% in the curated subset), repolarisation-rebound amplitude (median simulated/observed ratio 0.84) and frequency ordering (100\% of cells); the stricter per-cycle rebound criterion passes 45.8\% of 627 trials (Wilson 95\% CI 42--50\%; 59.1\% in the curated subset) and is registered, not sealed, for structural reasons given in SI S2. Across a five-temperature series (25/27/30/33/37 $^{\circ}$C; five further batches, 459 wells, same pipeline^19^), both population signatures held at every temperature: per-cell $E_{\mathrm{rev}}$ remained cell-invariant (CV $\le$ 0.034 at each temperature) and shifted from $-83.9$ mV at 25 $^{\circ}$C to $-92.0$ mV at 37 $^{\circ}$C, the latter within 1 mV of the model62 reference formulation's fitted value of $-92.9$ mV (SI S3.0; an independent convention, pre-registered as not mutually binding with the sealed $-88.33$ mV), and the negative inactivation limb stayed cell-invariant (CV $\le$ 0.08). The recovery time constant obeyed a single temperature law ($Q_{10}$ = 2.84, pooled $-140$/$-120$ mV; Arrhenius $R^2$ = 0.991), and at 37 $^{\circ}$C the CHO-cell median $\tau_{\mathrm{rec}}(-120 \mathrm{mV})$ of 1.52 ms agreed with the construction-set reference of 3.04 ms within the pre-registered factor-3 band (ratio 0.50, SI S2). The per-cell extraction underlying these population verdicts is deposited as a single master table of 18,353 cell-level parameter values for hERG across the six automated-patch batches, with rule-named per-voltage exclusion bookkeeping (SI S7; Extended Data Figs. 7, 8).

**Drug action on hERG is amplitude-type, not kinetic**

For all 30 drugs in the CiPA panel we tested whether drug action can be described as pure pore block (a rescaling of G). The pure-block hypothesis is rejected in every case: the cross-protocol discrepancy under a block-only model ($\varepsilon_{x}$ = 0.088) exceeds the pre-registered acceptance threshold (0.030) for 30 of 30 drugs (Methods; SI S4). Instead, drug action is consistently amplitude-type: it rescales the steady-state availability table ($h_{\mathrm{ss}}$) while leaving the deactivation and activation time-constant tables unmoved (steady-state shape deviation $|\kappa^{*} - 1|$ = 0.020 against a 0.10 threshold; temporal warping absorbs only $\approx15$\% of the deformation; Fig. 2e). In the $\alpha$-model vocabulary, hERG pharmacology is a change of occupancy, not of kinetics.

**Across four channels, time constants travel and midpoints do not**

We then applied the same pipeline to $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$, $Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$ and $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$, using published protocol-rich datasets for each (Methods). The portability verdicts are summarised in Fig. 3a and share one structure: time-constant tables, where they can be anchored at all, are constant across cells; steady-state midpoints are per-cell quantities with a few-millivolt biological spread; and the per-cell free-parameter count of each assembled model is one (conductance) or two (conductance plus one midpoint shift).

For **$Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$**,

$$I = G\, m(t)^3\, h(t)\, (V - E_{\mathrm{rev}}), \tag{2}$$

with $E_{\mathrm{rev}} \approx +72$--$+75$ mV from I--V fits (registered as weakly identified), the inactivation time-constant table $\tau_{\mathrm{h}}(V)$ (1.639 ms at $-30$ mV falling to 0.270 ms at +40 mV) is constant across cells, including at the threshold voltage $-30$ mV where an extrapolation test gives CV = 0.029. Steady-state inactivation has a constant slope ($k_{\mathrm{h}}$ = 4.79 mV) but a per-cell midpoint, $V_{1/2,\mathrm{h}}$ = $-66.1$ mV + $\Delta_{\mathrm{cell}}$ with $\Delta_{\mathrm{cell}}$ spread of $\pm4$ mV; the assembled model therefore frees exactly G and $\Delta V_{1/2,\mathrm{h}}$ per cell, and forward-predicts full held-out traces in 3 of 3 test cells (RMS error $\le$ 3.4\% of peak against a pre-registered 8\% threshold). The activation midpoint, by contrast, cannot be carried: series-resistance artefacts contaminate activation at the millisecond scale^9,15^, and we register $m_{\infty}$ as not portable rather than average it. The foot of the activation curve shows a genuine cell-to-cell spread (slope reference CV = 0.184), which our judgement cards record as biological variability, not measurement noise (SI S2).

**$Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ also produces the pipeline's cleanest negative result.** When we attempted to anchor the inactivation time constant at $-40$ mV across 69 multi-channel cell-attached recordings spanning constructs (WT, $\Delta$KPQ and $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.6$; ref. 21), the extracted values did not cluster: they form a broad distribution (median 1.03 ms; CV = 0.772; range 0.31--6.64 ms; Fig. 3b), and the extracted time constant correlates with the late-current fraction ($\rho$ = 0.44), marking the spread as modal-gating phenotype fluctuation rather than extraction noise. That reading is independently corroborated by the source study's own analysis of the same recordings^21^: $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ channels within a membrane patch do not gate independently: closed-state inter-channel interaction stabilises slow inactivation in a cluster-size-dependent manner, reducing late current and blunting use-dependent block. Patch-to-patch differences in cluster composition therefore make a broad $\tau_{\mathrm{h}}(-40)$ distribution the physical expectation rather than an anomaly. A further fraction is attributable to loss of voltage control in fast sodium recordings^9^, but the spread is not removable by quality filtering, and we therefore close this arm: $\tau_{\mathrm{h}}(-40)$ for $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ is to be cited as a distribution, and $I_{\mathrm{Na}}$ is not $\alpha$-ised in the host-model assembly below (it retains its published Markov form^14^). We report this as a quantitative property of the available $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ data, not as a modelling failure: it is precisely the kind of verdict that global fitting, which always returns a best-fit number, is structurally unable to deliver (Extended Data Fig. 3).

For **$Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$**, comparing Ba$^{2+}$ and Ca$^{2+}$ charge carriers isolates Ca$^{2+}$-dependent inactivation (CDI) as an additive fast component: the inactivation gate under Ca$^{2+}$ equals that under Ba$^{2+}$ minus a single saturating term,

$$h_{\mathrm{Ca}} = h_{\mathrm{Ba}} - w_{\mathrm{fast}}\bigl(1 - e^{-t/\tau_{\mathrm{f}}}\bigr), \tag{3}$$

with $w_{\mathrm{fast}} = 0.289$, independently corroborated by the steady-state inactivated-fraction difference between the two carriers ($\Delta f$ = 0.293) (Fig. 3c). Fast-component time constants span 6--12 ms; slow-component time constants are 50.0 ms (Ca$^{2+}$) and 41.6 ms (Ba$^{2+}$); the steady-state inactivated fraction at +17 mV in the characterization batch is 0.73 (Ca$^{2+}$) versus 0.44 (Ba$^{2+}$) (batch-anchored; cross-batch dispersion registered, SI S1.3a). Conductance temperature dependence is not a constant $Q_{10}$ across the measured range and is carried as an explicit covariate. The full $Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$ channel model is not assembled here because key activation-coverage protocols are absent from the available dataset; the CDI component structure above is data-complete and is what we carry forward (SI S1, S6; Extended Data Fig. 4).

For **$I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$** (KCNQ1+KCNE1), the current is

$$I = G\, a\, (V - E_{\mathrm{K}}), \qquad a = \beta\, a_{\mathrm{ss}} + (1 - \beta)\, a_{\mathrm{dyn}}, \tag{4}$$

with a channel-level pool constant $\beta = 0.30$. Activation is bell-shaped in voltage with a peak time constant of $\approx$ 8.9 s near $-10$ mV (author data table, ref. 16), accompanied by an explicit delay table (1.23 s at 0 mV to 0.50 s at +80 mV); the delay is not a fitting degree of freedom but the voltage-sensor/pore concerted step, matching the F2 fluorescence time constant of the same study at a 0.89 median ratio across five voltages (Extended Data Fig. 10). The activation model forward-predicts 7 of 8 held-out cells. Deactivation at $-40$ mV illustrates the judgement machinery again: the apparent (single-sweep) time constant is rejected as an artefact of incomplete recording windows, but the absolute magnitude (2--3 s) is anchored, and the residual cell-to-cell spread (CV = 0.313) is genuine biological variability. The model therefore carries the anchored magnitude with its measured dispersion, and only G is free per cell (Fig. 3d; SI S1; Extended Data Fig. 5).

**An identifiability audit explains why tables succeed where rate constants fail**

To make the comparison with the standard workflow concrete, we performed a Fisher-information audit of a 13-parameter Markov formulation of hERG taken from our own interim modelling chain (the model62 reference formulation, assembled from published components and fitted to the same four hERG protocol families; provenance, equations and calibration record in SI S3.0; Methods; Extended Data Fig. 2). The Fisher matrix has condition number $7.4 \times 10^{12}$ with six near-null eigendirections. Two degeneracies are exact: a joint rescaling of rates and conductance leaves all four protocols invariant, and the parameter combination $\alpha_{\mathrm{amp}} \cdot \mathrm{ALPHA} \cdot \mathrm{XMAX}$ enters only as a product. Projection onto the non-null subspace recovers exactly the quantities our pipeline extracts directly: the modal coordinates (time constants, steady-state levels, delays). A brute-force inversion test confirms that the mapping from rate constants to modal coordinates is not injective in the null directions (0 of 451 attempted loop inversions recover the generating point). The two constructions are therefore not rivals at the same address: the Markov parameterisation spans directions the data do not constrain, while the table-based model occupies only the identified subspace. A practical consequence for reporting is that rate constants quoted from such fits carry at least an order-of-magnitude uncertainty along the null directions, whereas the modal coordinates carry the measured cell-to-cell dispersion above (SI S3).

A second audit, on slow memory, bounds what any memoryless table model can express: at identical voltage, hERG currents differing only in voltage history differ by factors of 17--112 (9 of 9 cells), with a continuous spectrum of memory times from 4.8 s to beyond 2,000 s. Our models capture the dominant discrete modes of this spectrum explicitly (fast/middle/late components) but do not claim to reproduce its continuum; this boundary is stated in the model cards (SI S6).

**One drug-action grammar, channel-specific vocabularies**

We applied the same two drug tests, pure block versus steady-state modulation, to all four channels (Fig. 4). Pure conductance block is rejected everywhere (for $Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$: median cross-protocol discrepancy $\varepsilon_{x}$ = 0.223 against a null-calibrated threshold of 0.139; paired Wilcoxon p = $7.2 \times 10^{-16}$). What survives is a common grammar with channel-specific vocabularies:

- **hERG**: amplitude-type action on the availability table only ($|\kappa^{*} - 1|$ = 0.020; one component).
- **$Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$**: dual-component action, amplitude plus a temporal term ($|\kappa^{*}|$ = 0.20), quantitatively consistent with measured use dependence ($\Delta f$ = +0.12); across an external benchmark panel, all seven computable comparisons fall within a $\pm50$\% band (SI S4).
- **$Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$**: amplitude-type on average ($\kappa^{*}$ = $-0.01$) but with the two inactivation pools moving in opposite directions (inactivation-pool $\Delta f$ = +0.168; open-pool $\Delta f$ = $-0.088$), a bidirectional component invisible to any single-scalar description.
- **$I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$**: multi-table modulation, combining a batch-dependent shift of the activation steady state, an instantaneous component (+0.30) and slowed deactivation; the DIDS condition is pool-confounded in the source data and is reported as such rather than forced through the schema.

The unifying statement is that in all four channels drugs move steady-state occupancy and inactivation tables, not time-constant tables; what differs is the number and sign of separable action components. In the $\alpha$-model this has an immediate operational form: a drug is a documented edit to named tables, and "add one measured term" is consistently preferred over "re-fit a table" (SI S4).

**A fifth channel at registration grade: $K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$**

As a portability test outside the cardiac panel we ran the same pipeline on the only public protocol-rich $K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$ dataset: whole-cell recordings of the A-type current in identified substantia-nigra dopamine neurons (17 vehicle and 15 post-lesion cells, 37 $^{\circ}$C; ref. 22). Every anchor reported by the source study is reproduced by our extraction: peak current at $-30$ mV (3.95 $\pm$ 0.37 nA versus the published 3.8 $\pm$ 0.4 nA in controls; 1.95 $\pm$ 0.26 versus 1.7 $\pm$ 0.3 nA after 6-OHDA lesion), the approximately 50\% amplitude down-regulation, the selective change in the steady-state activation slope ($-28$\%), and the invariance of the availability midpoint and of the HCN current. The pattern seen in the four cardiac channels repeats here: the condition rewrites amplitude and activation slope and leaves the other measured tables unmoved. The extraction also surfaces a quantity the source study did not report. After within-protocol subtraction isolates the $K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$-attributed component, its inactivation time constant at $-20$ mV slows from 40.6 ms (n = 11) to 100.4 ms (n = 4) after lesion (+148\%; Mann--Whitney p = 0.001; interquartile ranges do not overlap). A series-resistance or clamp-quality explanation was tested and rejected: the time constant does not correlate with current amplitude within groups, and amplitude-matched cells still differ 2.4- to 3-fold across groups. Because the probe was not pre-registered, the post-lesion group is small, and the finding conflicts with the source study's statement that gating is unchanged apart from the activation slope, we register it as a candidate discovery awaiting new data rather than a sealed result (SI S10; Extended Data Fig. 9).

**Host-model validation: non-inferiority under the CiPA scoring pipeline**

As a terminal test we asked whether extracted components can serve inside the regulatory-grade host model. Two components passed assembly: hERG/$I_{\mathrm{Kr}}$ and $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$. In the O'Hara--Rudy human ventricular model^14^ we replaced the $I_{\mathrm{Kr}}$ and $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ Markov formulations with the $\alpha$-model tables, retained the published $I_{\mathrm{Na}}$ Markov model ($Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ arm closed, above), and retained the published $I_{\mathrm{CaL}}$ formulation ($Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$ not assembled, above). The composite passes all six host-level checks, including action-potential-duration agreement with the original model (APD90 difference 0.36\%) and retrospective trace agreement (difference $1.1 \times 10^{-5}$). The one substantive physiological difference is a 2.06-fold change in peak $I_{\mathrm{Kr}}$ during the action potential, which follows directly from the data-extracted time structure of the repolarising current and is flagged as the principal point of difference from the Markov form (the $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ peak ratio is 1.52 on a small baseline current; every other current agrees to within 2.1\%) (Fig. 5a, b; SI S5). We therefore do not claim quantitative identity of the repolarising currents; we claim that every disagreement is localised to a named, measured table.

For drug scoring we compared three arms under the CiPA dynamic-binding pipeline^2,3^ on the 24 drugs common to the 30-drug hERG library analysed above and the official CiPA risk-labelled list (7 high-, 11 intermediate- and 6 low-risk): the official Markov $I_{\mathrm{Kr}}$-dynamic model, and the $\alpha$-model $I_{\mathrm{Kr}}$ in static and dynamic binding modes. Under the dynamic-binding criterion the three arms score $-0.523$, $-0.547$ and (static) $-0.506$ respectively, with classification agreement 0.833--0.857 (Fig. 5c, d): the $\alpha$-model arm is non-inferior to the official arm under the dynamic-binding criterion. We emphasise the boundary of this claim: all arms share a performance ceiling near $-0.55$ under the 1 $\times$ Cmax static protocol, which is an attribute of the protocol and scoring data rather than of any model, and one compound (metoprolol) carries a data-side question after whose exclusion all arms pass (SI S5). All Stage-B statistics were recomputed from the deposited per-arm result files (dynamic-binding rows; Spearman's $\rho$ of qNet ratio against CiPA class; high-versus-low AUC); the recomputation reproduces the registered judgement-card values exactly ($\rho$ = $-0.5232$ / $-0.5470$ / $-0.5063$ for the official, $\alpha$-dynamic and $\alpha$-static arms; $\tau_{\mathrm{act}}$ sensitivity-arm range 0.016). A re-run of the qNet metric^3^ on the composite gives 0.998, unchanged from the official baseline.

### Discussion

The central claim of this work is not that any particular cardiac ion-channel model is wrong, but that the route by which the field obtains its parameters throws away information that modern datasets make directly measurable, and manufactures precision in directions the data do not constrain. When the same cells are taken through activation, inactivation, deactivation, sinusoidal and action-potential protocols in one session, the kinetic quantities that govern the current can be measured voltage by voltage, judged for constancy, and assembled into models whose per-cell free content is one or two numbers. Such models then forward-predict held-out protocols (65 of 68 CiPA screening traces for hERG with a single per-cell conductance) and, where they cannot be built, as for $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ at $-40$ mV, they return a measured distribution and a closed arm rather than a spurious point estimate. We regard the negative results as first-class products of the method: an audit trail that says where the data stop is precisely what a fit that always returns parameters cannot provide.

The cross-channel pattern of portable time constants and per-cell midpoints has a plausible physical reading. Time constants of gating are set by intramolecular energetics common to all copies of the channel protein, whereas steady-state midpoints integrate local factors (membrane environment, auxiliary-subunit stoichiometry, post-translational state) that vary from cell to cell. The data do not decide this reading, and we offer it as an interpretation, not a conclusion. What the data do establish is operational: any modelling practice that averages steady-state curves across cells is averaging over a few-millivolt biological coordinate, and for fast sodium currents such averaging biases are comparable to disease-mutation effect sizes^9^.

The identifiability audit reframes a long-standing practical observation. That independent fits return different rate constants has been noted repeatedly^6--8^; here we show, for a 13-parameter hERG formulation on four protocol families, that the unconstrained directions are six in number and include an exact rate--conductance degeneracy. The data-identified subspace is spanned by the modal coordinates, which is why direct extraction of those coordinates, with constancy judged before use, is not a shortcut but the correct parameterisation of the identified subspace. A corollary for the literature is that rate constants from global fits should be quoted with at least an order-of-magnitude uncertainty along null directions, while modal coordinates should be quoted with their measured cell-to-cell dispersion. One objection deserves a direct answer: that a table model is empirical where a state model is physical. The audit inverts this framing. Time constants, steady-state levels and delays are the observables of any gating scheme at the probed voltages; they are what the data identify, and the state rate constants along the unconstrained directions are the quantities with no empirical content. What the table model gives up is not physical meaning but unconstrained mechanism: state-level questions the data cannot decide are not answered, and extrapolation outside the measured voltage range follows an explicit, registered interpolation policy rather than an assumed state topology.

For drug-safety assessment the practical consequence is an audit trail. In the CiPA pipeline, channel pharmacology enters as a small set of numbers per drug per channel; in the present construction each such number is a documented edit to a named, measured table, and the pure-block hypothesis, which fails in all four channels, need never be assumed silently. The finding that drug action is steady-state modulation rather than kinetic modulation, with channel-specific component counts, suggests a compact and comparable vocabulary for cross-channel pharmacology that deserves testing on larger panels.

Several boundaries are stated plainly. $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ activation and low-voltage inactivation are limited by voltage-clamp artefacts^9,15^ and by genuine cross-construct dispersion; $Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$ awaits activation-coverage protocols for full assembly; $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ deactivation is anchored in magnitude but its apparent per-sweep values are recording-window artefacts; the single-variable steady state structurally undercounts true gating charge where limiting-slope measurements exist (by 1.3--1.8$\times$ in both $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ and hERG), and its valid window is bounded to $P_{\mathrm{o}} \gtrsim 0.01$, below which allosteric access to multiple open states lies outside the present claim (SI S1.4e); hERG slow memory includes a continuum of timescales that our discrete-component tables only partially express; hERG activation-head kinetics, which bound the AP-clamp peak magnitude, are carried as a registered band awaiting +50/+60 mV activation data (SI S1); and the host-model drug scoring shares the field's 1 $\times$ Cmax static-protocol ceiling. None of these boundaries is repaired by adding parameters. They are repaired by the experimental record, namely richer protocols at the voltages and durations that matter, and one function of the judgement-card machinery is to say exactly which records are missing. Read this way, the portability matrix of Fig. 3a doubles as a work list for any patch-clamp laboratory: each open entry names the protocol family whose absence leaves it open (activation-coverage protocols for $Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$; +50/+60 mV activation data for the hERG activation head), whereas the per-cell and distributed entries are answers rather than gaps, since no further recording will convert them into constants and they are to be measured cell by cell. The hERG construction rests on nine cells from a single study (the only manual-patch dataset with all six protocols in the same cells), so we quote exact confidence intervals and lean on cross-laboratory corroboration rather than on the construction set itself. The extension to automated-patch protocol-rich panels has now been completed for the population-level signatures (211 cells with nine protocols per cell^18^: per-cell $E_{\mathrm{rev}}$, the negative inactivation limb, the deactivation ladder ordering, and AP-clamp magnitude and frequency structure all reproduce; per-cycle rebound scoring and the mid-voltage inactivation limb remain registration-grade, SI S2). The temperature-matched series^19^ has now been completed: the absolute-timescale layer obeys a single $Q_{10}$ of 2.84 across 25--37 $^{\circ}$C (Arrhenius $R^2$ = 0.991) and agrees with the construction-set reference at 37 $^{\circ}$C (ratio 0.50, a factor of exactly two), while the slow deactivation component becomes window-limited at 37 $^{\circ}$C and is registered rather than sealed (SI S2; Extended Data Fig. 6). Finally, the CiPA panel extends beyond the four currents treated here. For $K_{\mathrm{ir}}2.1$ ($I_{\mathrm{K1}}$) no public raw-trace dataset exists, and for $K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$ ($I_{\mathrm{to}}$) exactly one exists; the registration-grade probe reported above used that single source, and cross-laboratory corroboration of the kind achieved for hERG awaits a second independent dataset (SI S9, S10).

More broadly, the extract--judge--assemble discipline is not specific to cardiac channels. Any voltage-gated channel recorded under protocol-rich conditions admits the same treatment, and the same three questions: which tables are constants of the molecule, which are coordinates of the cell, and what, precisely, do drugs edit. The framework connects to a general observation in biological systems: multiplicative scale is rarely calibratable from data, and what survives calibration failure is a small set of scale-free, measurable coordinates^17^. Here, those coordinates are the gating tables themselves.

## Methods

**Data sources.** Eight public dataset collections were used. (i) hERG: whole-cell recordings from nine CHO cells overexpressing hERG1a at 37 $^{\circ}$C under activation, inactivation, deactivation, sinusoidal, staircase and action-potential-clamp protocols^10^ (also archived at doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.4702546). (ii) $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$: fast sodium current recordings in HEK cells over-expressing $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ at 35 $^{\circ}$C under rupture-patch conditions with graded compensation levels^11^ (figshare doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.27193878), supplemented by the Tarasov cell-attached dataset^21^ (Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.0cfxpnwgh), from whose published extraction tables the $\tau_{\mathrm{h}}(-40)$ distribution was taken: 69 multi-channel patches spanning WT, $\Delta$KPQ and $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.6$ constructs carry the distribution statistics; the dataset's 14 single-channel patches and 3 whole-cell recordings are shown as reference overlays only (Extended Data Fig. 8d). (iii) $Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$: FDA $Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$ pharmacology dataset (Ba$^{2+}$ and Ca$^{2+}$ charge carriers, temperature series)^12^ (OSF g3msb). (iv) $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ kinetics: whole-cell and single-channel $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ activation data with voltage-sensor mutation series^16^ (J Gen Physiol 156(3), 2024; PMC10829594), with the activation time-constant and delay tables taken from the authors' data pack (Zenodo 10421153); our earlier figure-digitised versions were verified against that pack point-by-point ($\tau_{\mathrm{act}}$: 25 points, error $\le$ 2.2\%; delay: systematically low by 56--191 ms and replaced; SI S1.4, S1.4e). (v) $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ pharmacology: $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ activator dataset^13^ (Zenodo 8226585). (vi) hERG population validation: automated-patch recordings from 211 QC-passed CHO cells at 25 $^{\circ}$C under nine protocols (staircase-ramp, activation, inactivation, action-potential clamps at 0.5/1/2 Hz, and others)^18^, including the authors' 124-cell manually curated subset; we analysed the data-autoLC exports and applied the authors' own re-leak-correction step^18^ before all measurements. For the temperature layer we additionally analysed the companion temperature series^19^ (127 wells at 27 $^{\circ}$C, 118 at 30 $^{\circ}$C, 109 at 33 $^{\circ}$C, and 69 + 36 wells in two batches at 37 $^{\circ}$C; same host, QC and re-leak pipeline; staircase-ramp, activation and inactivation protocols at every temperature, plus action-potential clamps at 37 $^{\circ}$C), extracted with identical code under a separately frozen pre-registered judgement card. (vii) $K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$: whole-cell voltage-clamp recordings of A-type currents in identified substantia-nigra dopamine neurons at 37 $^{\circ}$C (activation, availability and HCN protocols; 17 vehicle and 15 6-OHDA-lesion cells)^22^ (Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.76hdr7t6z). CiPA drug-screening traces for hERG were taken from three contributing laboratories as distributed within the CiPA in silico validation materials accompanying refs 2, 3 (risk labels and free-Cmax table: official newCiPA.csv; SHA-256 on record with the deposited judgement cards).

**Extraction.** Each protocol family was reduced to tables by local measurement: exponential fits to activation, inactivation, deactivation and tail segments (mono- or bi-exponential as pre-registered per channel); Boltzmann fits to steady-state activation and availability; delay estimation by back-extrapolation of exponential fits to the current onset; reversal potentials from tail-current I--V crossings. No objective function coupling multiple protocols or multiple parameters was optimised at any stage. Full extraction code and per-sweep provenance are available (Code availability).

**Judgement cards.** Before assembly, every table received a pre-registered verdict: *constant* (cell-to-cell dispersion statistically indistinguishable from measurement noise; carried as a channel-level table), *per-cell* (dispersion significant and structured; carried as a per-cell coordinate), *distributed* (no stable central value; carried as median plus measured dispersion), or *unusable* (contaminated by a named artefact; excluded with reason recorded). Verdicts were fixed before any held-out prediction was attempted. Temperature was treated as an explicit covariate where datasets span temperatures ($Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$); quality filters for $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ included series-resistance and voltage-control criteria^9,15^.

**$K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$ probe (registration grade).** The $K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$ arm was analysed as a registration-grade probe without pre-registration (SI S10). HEKA-format traces were read directly; 18 of 28 activation files contained a corrupted repeat series, repaired by a rule fixed before any group comparison (at each voltage, if two repeats differ by more than a factor of five, the smaller-magnitude sweep is kept); currents were converted to nA before fitting. Group comparisons are two-sided Mann--Whitney tests. The $K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$-attributed component was isolated by within-protocol subtraction of the $-30$ mV-conditioned trace from the $-120$ mV-conditioned trace. The series-resistance confound test used within-group Spearman correlations between current amplitude and extracted time constant, together with direct comparison of amplitude-matched cells across groups.

**Assembly and forward prediction.** Assembled currents use the Hodgkin--Huxley-form equations of SI S1 (Eqs. (S1)--(S12)) with the accepted tables inserted. Per-cell free parameters were: hERG, G only; $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$, G and $\Delta V_{1/2,\mathrm{h}}$; $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$, G only. Forward predictions were scored against held-out protocols and cells at pre-registered accuracy thresholds (SI S2), including sinusoidal and action-potential-clamp prediction (hERG), full-trace prediction ($Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$), activation prediction ($I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$) and CiPA trace reproduction (hERG, 68 traces, three laboratories). Residual whiteness was assessed by a Ljung--Box-type test (SI S2).

**Identifiability audit.** For the reference 13-parameter hERG Markov formulation, the Fisher information matrix was computed at the published best-fit point over the four protocol families by automatic differentiation of the log-likelihood; eigenspectra, condition number, and null-direction composition are reported in SI S3. The non-injectivity test attempted 451 loop inversions sampling the null manifold. Slow-memory spectra were estimated by history-dependent current ratios at identical voltages (SI S3).

**Drug-action metrics.** Two pre-registered shape metrics were computed per drug per channel (SI S4): $\varepsilon_{x}$, the cross-protocol discrepancy under a pure-conductance-block model (acceptance threshold 0.030), and $\kappa^{*}$, the steady-state shape deviation under a single-scalar rescaling. Component attributions (amplitude vs temporal; pool-resolved for $Ca_{\mathrm{v}}1.2$) follow the decision tree in SI S4. Use-dependence consistency for $Na_{\mathrm{v}}1.5$ was checked against measured $\Delta f$.

**Host-model integration.** $\alpha$-model $I_{\mathrm{Kr}}$ and $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ components were substituted into the O'Hara--Rudy human ventricular myocyte model^14^; $I_{\mathrm{Na}}$ retained the published Markov form and $I_{\mathrm{CaL}}$ the published formulation (registry of substitutions in SI S5). Host-level checks J1--J6 include APD90 agreement, retrospective trace agreement, and current time-structure comparison. Drug scoring used the CiPA dynamic-binding pipeline^2,3^ with the official $I_{\mathrm{Kr}}$-dynamic model as reference arm; classification agreement and qNet were computed as described^3^. The 1 $\times$ Cmax static-protocol ceiling was assessed by scoring all arms under the static protocol; the metoprolol data-side question is documented in SI S5.

**Statistics.** Dispersions are reported as coefficients of variation across cells; distributional quantities as median, CV and range. Hypothesis tests (pure-block rejection; whiteness) were two-sided at the pre-registered thresholds. Sample sizes are the numbers of cells/traces stated per result; no data were excluded after verdict registration. Binomial confidence intervals are Wilson score intervals. Measurement-level exclusions (per cell, per voltage step, per sweep) follow pre-registered rules and are tabulated in a 90-entry exclusion registry spanning five categories (data quality, protocol limitation, method boundary, forward-judgement failure and registered real physics; SI S7).

**Data availability.** All source datasets are public: hERG^10^ (figshare doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.4702546), Nav1.5^11^ (figshare doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.27193878), CaV1.2^12^ (OSF g3msb), $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ kinetics^16^ (PMC10829594), $I_{\mathrm{Ks}}$ pharmacology^13^ (Zenodo 8226585), the hERG population panel and five-temperature series^18,19^ (github.com/CardiacModelling/hERGRapidCharacterisation), the CiPA multi-laboratory drug-screening archive^2,3^, and the $K_{\mathrm{v}}4.3$ substantia-nigra dataset^22^ (Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.76hdr7t6z). Derived artefacts deposited with this work include the four-channel per-cell parameter master table (18,710 rows), the exclusion registry (90 entries) and the third-party literature anchor layer (SI S7, S8); derived tables, judgement cards and model cards accompany the SI.

**Code availability.** Extraction, judgement, assembly, audit and host-integration code, together with the per-cell parameter tables, judgement cards and figure-generation scripts, is available at github.com/lihuanlz/alpha-ion-channel-models (archived at Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23008849, tag v05).
